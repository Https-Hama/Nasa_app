# HUMAN.exe Human Observatory play guide

## Launch

Extract the complete ZIP and double-click START_HUMAN_EXE.cmd. Node.js 22 or newer is required. The production game needs no internet connection after extraction. Keep the launcher terminal open and use its localhost URL. Ctrl+C stops that server.

Save your name before your first mission. On a new visit, confirm the player remembered by this browser, choose a saved player or enter a new name. The app remembers the selected browser profile; it does not recognize faces. Select the name in the top right to rename a profile or switch players when a mission is not active. Naming the original Local player preserves its existing records.

## Camera controls

Select Enable camera controls and accept the browser permission prompt. For portrait or seated play, hold a relaxed neutral pose for two seconds with your shoulders, arms and both hands visible. Your hips and legs can stay outside the frame. Recalibrate after changing camera distance or position. Use a comfortable, clear space; full-body framing is only needed for leg actions.

Move the left hand to aim. Extend the right arm at shoulder height to fire repeatedly; raise both hands overhead to shield. A completed knee lift followed by lowering triggers a short boost. A completed squat-and-rise cycle triggers a brief duck. Return to neutral between leg actions; held poses do not repeatedly trigger them. These are game-control thresholds, not an exercise-form assessment.

WASD or arrows aim with keyboard controls. Hold J to fire. Space shields; Shift boosts; C ducks; P pauses. Brief camera-tracking gaps clear the previous movement and firing inputs without pausing play. Sustained tracking loss temporarily pauses the task and resumes automatically when your shoulders and hands return. Losing window focus alone does not pause. Switching to a hidden tab, manually pausing or opening another panel requires a manual resume. A disconnected camera releases the stream and restores keyboard fallback. Controls are only read for the focused game or active mind-check dialog.

## Face and body observations

Observation consent is separate from camera permission. Check Enable face & body observations to load the local face model. Select Start 30-second face check. Move closer so your face is well lit and fills enough of the preview. Follow the live guidance for a small or missing face, low light and open-eye calibration progress. Relax and look toward the camera; there is no need to perform facial expressions. An open-eye calibration period comes first. Early checks are labeled with their actual duration for comparison.

Stay close for portrait play with your shoulders and hands visible, or step back if you want leg actions. Recalibrate after moving, then launch the flight. The close-up face measurements remain separate from intentional flight movements; distant flight framing does not erase a completed face calibration. Portrait play can still record hand movement when leg measurements are unavailable. Faces that are too small, dark, poorly oriented or poorly sampled produce unavailable measurements without blocking the game. Withdrawing consent ends an active check immediately and cancels pending observation loading.

Completed and early face checks save to the selected player's independent face-check archive even when you do not launch a flight. Mission health shows the number of archived checks and includes them in its history export. An old archived check is not automatically reused for a new mission. No video, images or raw landmarks are saved.

## Combat and mind checks

The single two-minute flight has actual enemy drones, laser fire, drone attacks, asteroid impacts, shields, boosts and ducking. Protect the hull and fire along the flight path. After the flight ends, the mind check opens automatically.

Memory starts with a newly randomized three-direction pattern for each check. Each successful round adds a random direction, giving rounds of three, four and five directions. Watch the illuminated tiles, then repeat the pattern using arrows or taps. Body control can aim toward a direction and extend the arm to confirm. Incorrect input records an error and replays the current pattern.

The focus task shuffles eight cues for each check while preserving the same GO/WAIT mix for consistent difficulty. Respond to cyan GO with Space, the Respond button or arm extension; do not respond to orange WAIT. P pauses/resumes. Tracking gaps use the same grace and automatic recovery as flight; hidden tabs and manual pauses require you to resume. Skip to report is available; partial task results remain visible but are not compared with completed tasks. Keyboard or tap use during a motion session marks mixed input and disables longitudinal comparison for that record.

## Journal and comparison

Use Mission health for daily manual readings, warning explanations and record export. Check-ins, diagnosis notes, preferences, face-check summaries and mission reports belong to the selected player and save automatically. Mission progress saves every five seconds, after task inputs and when the page is hidden or left. Both partial and completed reports remain in the journal. Reloading preserves the latest saved partial report; it does not restart that interrupted mission.

The Health journal selects a current session and an earlier comparator. It prefers the newest usable comparable record and shows unavailable deltas with reasons. Quality gates, task protocol, duration and input mode prevent comparisons between different conditions, including older fixed-pattern and newer randomized tasks. Labels use Asia/Baghdad calendar dates.

Demo preview always carries a FICTIONAL RECORDS banner and contains two synthetic sessions for yesterday/today. These records are never mixed with measured reports or silently stored. Demo exports are also labeled fictional. Export report saves derived JSON; Delete saved reports removes measured browser and in-memory records, not separately exported files.

Mission health exports the selected player's check-ins, mission reports and independent face-check archive together. Use the same browser profile and exact localhost address, including the port, to find saved data again. Different browsers or addresses use separate storage. Export a backup before clearing site data. If a storage warning appears, keep the page open and retry saving or export the available records before closing. Browser storage is local to this device and is not an encrypted medical record system. Video, images and raw landmarks are never included in saved records.

## Meaning and limits

The detailed report describes visible events and task performance. It is not a medical diagnosis, clinical cognitive test, overall health score or evidence of fatigue, neurological disease, emotion, bone loss or muscle weakness. Camera motion does not supply astronaut resistance training. Suggested next steps concern camera conditions, repeated comparable sessions, comfortable breaks and qualified clinical review of persistent concerns.

Real-person camera accuracy and performance on your particular hardware still need a playtest. Automated synthetic landmarks and a synthetic camera verify implementation behavior, not clinical validity or human accuracy.

## Gemini mission reviews

After finishing the flight and mind check, the report opens immediately and Gemini automatically reviews its anonymous derived mission summary. A loading panel becomes the written review, which saves with the selected player’s report. If the service fails, the report and charts remain available and Retry Gemini review can try again. Existing completed reports can use Generate Gemini review; partial progress and fictional previews do not upload.

Automatic Gemini mission reviews can be disabled in Preferences. When enabled, task scores, flight results and usable derived camera measurements are sent to Google. Names, video, images, raw landmarks, manual check-ins and diagnosis notes are excluded. The review describes this session and practical play conditions; it is not a medical assessment.

The four new charts show task accuracy, response counts, camera coverage and usable hand travel. Hover or focus a chart for an explanation, or expand its values table. N/A means no usable data. Charts respect the device’s reduced-motion preference.
